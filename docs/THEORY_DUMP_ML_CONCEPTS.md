### Pillar 3: Why Was Plain 1D-CNN Inferior (71.37%) vs. ResNet (89.29%–91.08%)?
* **The Receptive Field & Pooling Dilemma:** An ECG has two conflicting features: a razor-sharp high-frequency spike (QRS: 15–35 samples) and slow low-frequency domes (P/T waves: 50–90 samples). Plain CNNs use successive MaxPool layers ($300 \to 150 \to 75$). This rounds off the sharp peak and obliterates the exact slope ($dV/dt$), discarding the unique fiducial timings that distinguish individuals.
* **The Degradation Problem & Vanishing Gradients:** In plain CNNs, the backpropagated gradient shrinks exponentially through deep layers ($\lim_{L \to \text{deep}} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_1} \approx 0$). Early filters stop learning and stay quasi-random.
* **The ResNet Identity Superpower:** Formulating the block as $\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$ gives the backpropagation gradient:
  $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial \mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{I} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}$$
  The $+\mathbf{I}$ term creates an **unobstructed gradient highway**. Even if the conv layer gradient diminishes, gradients flow backwards unhindered. High-frequency QRS details pass directly to the final layer!

### Pillar 4: Metric Learning vs. Standard Classification (ArcFace vs. Softmax)
* **Why Softmax Fails for Real-World Biometrics:** Softmax cross-entropy requires a fixed number of classes $N$. In a real system, you cannot retrain a neural network on the cloud every time a new user enrolls. Furthermore, Softmax only separates classes linearly—it does not pull same-person embeddings together.
* **The ArcFace Solution:** ArcFace maps all embeddings to a unit hypersphere ($||\mathbf{x}||=1, ||\mathbf{w}||=1$). During training, it forces an **additive angular penalty** $m = 0.35\text{ rad} \approx 20^\circ$ on the target class angle:
  $$\cos(\theta + m) = \cos(\theta)\cos(m) - \sin(\theta)\sin(m)$$
* **The Result:** The model is forced to pull same-person heartbeats into an ultra-tight cone on the sphere, while pushing different people at least $20^\circ$ away. Enrolling a new user simply stores their 48-D vector; verification is a fast cosine dot product $\mathbf{e}_{\text{live}} \cdot \mathbf{e}_{\text{enrolled}} \ge 0.74$.

### Pillar 5: Why the Dual-Stream Architecture ($[x[n], \Delta x[n]]$)?
* **Stream 1 ($x[n]$):** Voltage amplitude (reflects static heart muscle mass, chamber size, and chest thickness).
* **Stream 2 ($\Delta x[n] = x[n] - x[n-1]$):** Time-derivative $dV/dt$ (reflects dynamic conduction velocity across the Purkinje fibers).
* **Why it matters:** Two individuals with similar body builds may have identical voltage peaks, but their Purkinje action potential speeds are distinct. Feeding both streams allows the 1st-stage conv filters to extract joint spatio-temporal dynamics.

### Pillar 6: Multi-Beat Voting: Pushing Accuracy Past 97%
* Single beats fluctuate due to **Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia (RSA)** (breathing alters heart rate and thoracic impedance) and brief muscle tremors.
* When averaging $K$ consecutive beat embeddings:
  $$\bar{\mathbf{e}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{e}_k}{||\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{e}_k||}$$
  The true individual identity vector points in the same direction and adds constructively ($\propto K$), while random physiological noise vectors cancel out ($\propto \sqrt{K}$).
* Under the Binomial distribution, the probability of error drops exponentially:
  * **1 Beat:** 91.08%
  * **3 Beats:** 94.61%
  * **5 Beats:** 96.63%
  * **8 Beats:** **97.19%** *(Crosses the 97% mark)*
  * **10 Beats:** **97.75%**

### Pillar 7: Hardware Deployment: Edge vs. Frontier Trade-Off
* **Why deploy Single-Stream `ECG_ResNet_SE` to the RP2040?**
  * **SRAM Footprint:** 72 KB static activation buffers fit comfortably within the RP2040's 264 KB SRAM (leaving 100+ KB free for stack, heap, and ring buffers). Dual-Stream requires 97 KB.
  * **Inference Latency:** 300.05 ms per beat on the Cortex-M0+ (using soft-float emulation). At 75 BPM, Core 1 sleeps 70% of the time.
  * **5-Beat Unlock:** 95.51% verification in only 4.0 seconds.
* **Dual-Stream** serves as the high-accuracy research frontier (97.19%–97.75%) suitable for edge-gateways or cloud backends.

## 1. Foundational Intuition: What is ECG and Why Can It Identify People?

### 1.1 The Heart as an Electrical Generator
Imagine your heart not as a mechanical pump, but as an **electrically-timed biological engine**. Every contraction begins with an electrical wave generated by specialized pacemaker cells:

1. **Sinoatrial (SA) Node Fire:** The biological spark plug in the upper right atrium fires automatically. Electricity spreads across both atria, causing them to squeeze blood into the ventricles $\to$ **P-Wave** (atrial depolarization).
2. **Atrioventricular (AV) Node Delay:** The electrical impulse reaches the AV node, which acts like a biological resistor. It pauses the signal for ~100 ms to give blood enough time to drain into the lower chambers before they pump.
3. **Purkinje Fiber Discharge:** The impulse breaks out of the AV node and rushes at incredible speeds down the bundle branches and Purkinje fiber network. This triggers massive, instantaneous ventricular contraction $\to$ **QRS Complex** (tall, sharp electrical spike).
4. **Ventricular Recovery:** The ventricles recharge electrically to prepare for the next beat $\to$ **T-Wave** (ventricular repolarization).

```
   Voltage (mV)
       ^
       |                R  (Peak electrical depolarization of ventricles)
       |               / \
       |              /   \
       |      P      /     \           T  (Ventricular repolarization)
       |    _--_    /       \         _---_
       |  -'    '- /         \      -'     '-
  -----+----------Q-----------S----+--------------> Time (samples / ms)
       |                      
       |                      <---- ST Segment ---->
       |                      <------- QT Interval ------->
```

### 1.2 The "Key & Lock" Analogy: Why is Your ECG Biometrically Unique?
> **Mental Model:** A person's face can be altered by makeup, aging, or surgery. A fingerprint can be abraded or lifted with tape. But your ECG is shaped by the physical architecture of an internal organ that you cannot voluntarily alter.

No two people on Earth have the exact same ECG because of four unalterable anatomical factors:
1. **Heart Mass & Geometry:** The left ventricle is the main pumping chamber. Its wall thickness, volume, and physical orientation (the tilt of the heart's electrical axis relative to your ribs) create an idiosyncratic electrical vector.
2. **Thoracic Impedance:** The electricity must pass from heart tissue through pericardial fluid, lungs, ribs, subcutaneous chest fat, and skin to reach the electrodes. A marathon runner with 8% body fat will project an electrical wave with different attenuation and high-frequency harmonics than an average individual.
3. **Purkinje Conduction Speed:** The density and branching geometry of Purkinje fibers determine how fast the depolarization wave front travels ($dV/dt$). This electrical conduction speed is genetically and developmentally determined.
4. **Inherent Liveness Detection:** A dead finger or silicone mold can fool a capacitive fingerprint scanner. A photo or deepfake video can fool a camera. But a biometric system that measures cardiac depolarization **cannot be spoofed without a living human heart**.

## 2. The Big Picture: What Problem Are We Solving? (Open-Set vs. Closed-Set)

In academic ML tutorials (like MNIST digit classification), you are given 10 fixed classes ($0\dots9$). The model's job is simple: *"Which of these 10 known classes does this image belong to?"*  
This is called a **Closed-Set Problem**.

```
   Closed-Set (Academic MNIST):
   Input Image ---> [ Neural Network ] ---> [ Softmax Layer: 10 fixed outputs ] ---> "It's a 7!"
   
   Open-Set Biometric Reality (Real World):
   Alice enrolls today  ---> Vector e_Alice stored in DB
   Bob enrolls tomorrow ---> Vector e_Bob stored in DB
   Charlie walks in     ---> Is Charlie Alice? Or Bob? Or a complete stranger?
```

In real-world Biometric Systems, closed-set classification is **completely useless**:
1. **You cannot retrain the network:** When a company hires a new employee, you cannot re-run a 48-hour PyTorch training loop on an AWS cluster to add an 11th output class!
2. **The Open-Set Challenge:** The network must be trained on a benchmark population (e.g., 40 subjects) to learn *what human heartbeats look like in general*, and then deployed in a mode where it can identify or verify **brand new people it has never seen during training**.
3. **The Solution (Hyperspherical Metric Learning):** Instead of learning classification boundaries, the neural network acts as a **Feature Extractor**. It transforms a 300-sample raw ECG beat into a compact **48-dimensional normalized embedding vector** ($\mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^{48}, ||\mathbf{e}||=1$).
   - If two beats are from the **same person**, their vectors point in nearly identical directions ($\cos\theta \approx 1.0$).
   - If two beats are from **different people**, their vectors point far apart ($\cos\theta \approx 0.0$).

### Detailed Breakdown of Every Stage:

#### Stage 1: Classical AC/DCT + SVM (79.06%)
* **Hypothesis:** Autocorrelation (AC) removes heart-rate variability by measuring signal self-similarity across time lags. Taking the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) of the autocorrelation extracts the 32 most energetic frequency coefficients, which an SVM can classify.
* **Why It Plateaued at 79%:** Autocorrelation computes $\hat{R}_{xx}[m] = \sum x[n]x[n+m]$. In doing so, it completely **discards the relative phase between waves**! In cardiology, the exact time delay between the P-wave and the R-peak (the PR interval) is a critical individual biometric marker. AC threw it away. An SVM cannot recover information that the preprocessing discarded.

#### Stage 2: Recurrent Neural Network / LSTM (49.17%)
* **Hypothesis:** ECG is a time series, and LSTMs excel at time series. Let's feed the 32 DCT coefficients into an LSTM.
* **Why It Failed Catastrophically (49.17%):** LSTMs assume that step $t$ causally follows step $t-1$ (like words in a sentence). But DCT coefficients are **orthogonal frequency bins**, not a temporal sequence! Treating frequency bin 2 as "happening after" frequency bin 1 was fundamentally flawed. Furthermore, when LSTMs are fed raw waveforms, recurrent unrolling over 300 timesteps causes severe gradient vanishing without local spatial filtering.

#### Stage 3: Plain 1D-CNN (71.37%)
* **Hypothesis:** Convolutions naturally learn translation-invariant spatial patterns (P, QRS, T wave shapes).
* **Why It Hit a Ceiling at 71.37%:** Fixed receptive fields and aggressive pooling ($300 \to 150 \to 75$) destroyed high-frequency QRS details, while gradient vanishing prevented deep layers from refining features. *(Detailed in Section 4)*.

#### Stage 4: 1D-ResNet + ArcFace Metric Learning (89.29%)
* **Hypothesis:** Add identity skip connections ($\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$) to create a gradient highway, and replace closed-set Softmax with hyperspherical ArcFace margin loss.
* **Result:** **Massive +17.92% leap!** Features from early layers flowed unhindered to the output, and ArcFace forced same-person beats into tight angular clusters. *(Detailed in Sections 5 & 7)*.

#### Stage 5: ResNet-SE with Channel Attention (90.87%)
* **Hypothesis:** Not all feature channels are equally important. Let the network dynamically amplify channels tracking the sharp R-peak while muting channels contaminated by sensor baseline drift.
* **Result:** **+1.58% boost**, surpassing 90% single-beat accuracy. *(Detailed in Section 6)*.

#### Stage 6: Dual-Stream ResNet ($[x[n], \Delta x[n]]$) (91.08% $\to$ 97.19% $\to$ 97.75%)
* **Hypothesis:** Supplement raw voltage $x[n]$ with its first-order discrete difference $\Delta x[n] = x[n] - x[n-1]$ to directly provide Purkinje conduction velocity ($dV/dt$).
* **Result:** Single-beat climbed to **91.08%**. Aggregating across 8 beats pushed accuracy beyond 97% to **97.19%**, and 10 beats reached **97.75%**. *(Detailed in Sections 8 & 9)*.

## 4. Deep Dive 1: Why Was the Plain 1D-CNN Inferior?

In Stage 3, we built a standard 3-layer 1D-CNN (`ECG_CNN`):
- Conv1D ($1 \to 16, k=15$) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool(2)
- Conv1D ($16 \to 32, k=9$) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool(2)
- Conv1D ($32 \to 32, k=5$) $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ AdaptiveAvgPool(1)

It scored **71.37%**. Why did it fail so badly?

```
               HOW MAXPOOLING DESTROYS BIOMETRIC UNIQUENESS
               
    Original High-Res Beat Window:          After Layer 1 & 2 MaxPool:
                 R                                     R (Rounded!)
                / \                                   / \
               /   \                                 /   \
              /     \                               *     *
             /       \                             /       \
            /         \                           *         *
    -------Q-----------S-------          --------*-----------*-------
    (Sharp, distinct slopes &            (Coarse, sub-sampled grid:
     precise fiducial timings!)           differences between people erased!)
```

### 4.1 The Receptive Field Dilemma (Multi-Scale Conflict)
> **The Telescope Analogy:** Imagine trying to take a picture that clearly shows both a tiny ant on a leaf and a wide mountain range in the background. If your lens is zoomed in on the ant, the mountain is invisible. If you zoom out to see the mountain, the ant is a blurry speck.

An ECG heartbeat has the exact same problem:
- **The Ant (QRS Complex):** A violent, 40 ms spike (15–30 samples). To capture the exact slope ($dV/dt$) and notch variations that identify Person A, the network needs **small, high-resolution filters**.
- **The Mountain (P and T Waves):** Broad, smooth domes lasting 150–250 ms (50–90 samples). To capture atrial mass and repolarization, the network needs **wide receptive fields**.

In a plain CNN, the only way to get a wide receptive field is to apply **MaxPooling** (sub-sampling by picking the maximum value in every 2-sample window).  
**The Tragedy:** MaxPool cuts temporal resolution in half at every layer ($300 \to 150 \to 75$). It rounds off the sharp needle-point of the R-peak and smears the S-wave slope! The deep layers receive a blurry, low-resolution abstraction. Two completely different people whose ECGs only differ in the sharpness of their QRS upstrokes end up looking identical to the network.

### 4.2 The Degradation Problem & The "Telephone Game" of Gradients
> **The Telephone Game Analogy:** When a message is whispered down a line of 10 people, by the time it reaches the 10th person, the words are garbled and quiet.

During neural network training, the "learning signal" (gradient) is whispered backwards from the loss function at the output back to the early layers:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_1} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_L} \prod_{k=2}^L \mathbf{W}_k^T \cdot \sigma'(\mathbf{z}_k)$$
In a plain CNN without shortcuts:
- Every time the gradient passes through a weight matrix $\mathbf{W}_k$ and a non-linear activation derivative $\sigma'$, it gets multiplied by numbers smaller than 1.
- In deep networks, the gradient shrinks exponentially until $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}_1} \approx 0$.
- **Result:** The earliest convolutional layers (which are supposed to detect the crisp edges of the QRS wave) stop learning completely. Their weights remain semi-random noise!

## 5. Deep Dive 2: Why Did ResNet Surpass Everything?

In 2015, Kaiming He et al. solved the degradation problem with **Residual Networks (ResNet)**. Adapting this for 1D biomedical signals propelled our accuracy to **89.29%**.

```
                Plain CNN (Traffic Jam)             ResNet (Express Highway)
                
                     x                                            x
                     │                                        ┌───┴───┐
                     ▼                                        │       │ (Express Bypass)
             [ Conv1D + BN ]                                  │       ▼
                     │                                        │  [ Conv1D + BN ]
                     ▼                                        │       │
                  [ ReLU ]                                    │       ▼
                     │                                        │   [ ReLU ]
                     ▼                                        │       │
             [ Conv1D + BN ]                                  │       ▼
                     │                                        │  [ Conv1D + BN ]
                     ▼                                        │       │
                  Output                                      └───►(+)  (Add Input x back!)
                                                                      │
                                                                      ▼
                                                                   [ ReLU ]
```

### 5.1 The Mathematical Superpower: The Identity Highway
In a standard network, the layers must learn the complete transformation $\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})$.  
In a Residual Block, we break the transformation into two parts:
$$\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$$
$$\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{y} - \mathbf{x} \quad \text{(The Residual / Difference)}$$

Now observe what happens to the gradient during backpropagation!
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathbf{y}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial \mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{I} \right)$$
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial \mathbf{x}}}_{\text{Conv Layer Path}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{I} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}}_{\text{UNOBSTRUCTED IDENTITY HIGHWAY}}$$

Notice the term $\mathbf{I} \cdot \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}$!
- Even if the convolutional layers suffer catastrophic vanishing gradients ($\frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \to 0$), the gradient **flows straight through the identity highway ($\mathbf{I}$)** without losing a single bit of energy!
- Early layers continue to receive strong, crisp learning signals throughout all 40 training epochs.
- Furthermore, the input $\mathbf{x}$ (which contains the raw, un-blurred high-frequency QRS spike) is **directly added** to the output. The sharp features never get lost!

### 5.2 Depthwise-Separable Convolutions: The "Master Chef" Optimization
Standard convolutions are computationally gluttonous. For $C_{in}=24, C_{out}=24, K=7$:
$$\text{Standard Parameters} = 24 \times 24 \times 7 = 4,032\text{ weights}$$

> **The Chef Analogy:** In a poorly run kitchen, 24 chefs try to both chop vegetables and mix the sauce at the exact same table, constantly bumping into each other. A smart kitchen divides the work: Line Cook 1 chops the carrots; Chef 2 blends the sauce.

We implemented **Depthwise-Separable Convolutions**:
1. **Depthwise Conv (`groups = channels`):** Each of the 24 channels is filtered independently along the time axis using its own 1D kernel ($k=7$). Zero cross-channel mixing. ($24 \times 7 = 168$ weights).
2. **Pointwise Conv ($1 \times 1$):** A simple linear transformation mixes the 24 channels together. ($24 \times 24 \times 1 = 576$ weights).

$$\text{Total Separable Parameters} = 168 + 576 = \mathbf{744\text{ weights}}$$
$$\text{Parameter Reduction} = 1 - \frac{744}{4032} = \mathbf{81.5\%\text{ Slashing of Weights!}}$$
This single architectural optimization allowed our entire ResNet model to consume only **78.9 KB of Flash**, making deployment onto the 264 KB SRAM of the Raspberry Pi Pico trivial.

## 6. Deep Dive 3: Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Attention

In Stage 5, we added **Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks** to the residual stages, creating `ECG_ResNet_SE` (accuracy: **90.87%**).

```
                      x : (Batch, 24 Channels, 75 Length)
                                     │
                                     ▼
                      [ SQUEEZE: Global Average Pool ]  ───► Vector of shape (Batch, 24)
                                     │
                                     ▼
                      [ Dense Layer 1: 24 -> 6 (r=4) ]  ───► Bottleneck compression
                                     │
                                     ▼
                                 [ ReLU ]
                                     │
                                     ▼
                      [ Dense Layer 2: 6 -> 24 ]        ───► Restore channel count
                                     │
                                     ▼
                                [ Sigmoid ]             ───► Weights s_c in [0.0, 1.0]
                                     │
                                     └───────────────────────────────┐
                                                                     ▼
                                                        y = x * s   (Dynamic Recalibration!)
```

### 6.1 The Audio Equalizer Analogy
> **Mental Model:** When listening to music in a noisy car, you boost the mid-range vocals and dial down the rumbling bass road noise. An SE Block is an **automatic, real-time graphic equalizer for neural network channels**.

Standard convolution applies the same fixed weight to all 24 channels. But in cardiac biometrics:
- **Channel 5** might be specialized in detecting the sharp upward slew rate of the R-peak (carries massive personal identity information).
- **Channel 18** might be detecting the flat electrical baseline between the T-wave and the next P-wave (mostly contains low-frequency respiration wander or 50 Hz powerline hum).

### 6.2 The Mathematical Mechanism
1. **Squeeze Step (Global Summary):** We collapse the 75 time steps into a single scalar energy value for each channel $c$:
   $$z_c = \frac{1}{L} \sum_{t=1}^L x_c[t]$$
2. **Excitation Step (Non-Linear Inter-Channel Reasoning):** We feed this 24-dimensional energy vector into a two-layer bottleneck network (reduction ratio $r=4$):
   $$\mathbf{s} = \sigma\left(\mathbf{W}_2 \cdot \text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \cdot \mathbf{z})\right)$$
   Where $\mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 24}$, $\mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{24 \times 6}$, and $\sigma$ is the Sigmoid function ($\in [0, 1]$).
3. **Dynamic Scaling:** Every feature map channel is scaled by its personal importance weight:
   $$\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_c = s_c \cdot \mathbf{x}_c$$
If a heartbeat is noisy, the SE block automatically computes $s_{18} \approx 0.04$ (silencing the noise channel) and $s_5 \approx 0.97$ (amplifying the biometric R-peak channel).

## 7. Deep Dive 4: Metric Learning vs. Standard Classification (ArcFace vs. Softmax)

This is the single most critical ML theory concept for your presentation.

```
          Standard Softmax Space                        Metric Learning (ArcFace)
          
                Class A                                         Class A
                  * * *                                          * * *
                 *  *  *                                        *  *  *
                   * *                                            * *
              ------------- (Separating Plane)                    / \
                   * *                                           /   \ (Enforced Angular Margin)
                 *  *  *                                        /     \
                  * * *                                        * *     * *
                Class B                                      * * *     * * *
                                                            Class B    Class C
        [ Separable, but NOT compact. ]            [ Highly compact clusters on hypersphere; ]
        [ High intra-class variance!  ]            [ Huge angular margin between identities! ]
```

### 7.1 Why Standard Softmax Cross-Entropy Fails
In standard classification:
$$P(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{e^{\mathbf{w}_c^T \mathbf{x} + b_c}}{\sum_j e^{\mathbf{w}_j^T \mathbf{x} + b_j}}$$
1. **Softmax only demands separability, not compactness:** Softmax is happy as long as Class A is on one side of a line and Class B is on the other. It doesn't care if Person A's heartbeats are scattered all over the place. If Person A exercises and their heart rate rises, their embedding drifts right over the line.
2. **Magnitude Bias:** If a noisy heartbeat has a large voltage amplitude, its vector length $||\mathbf{x}||$ will be huge, dominating the dot product even if its shape is wrong.
3. **Closed-Set Trap:** When an 11th person wants to use the system, Softmax cannot handle them without retraining the whole model.

### 7.2 The ArcFace Solution: The "20-Degree Bouncer Rule"
> **The Bouncer Analogy:** Imagine a VIP club where patrons are seated in booths. Softmax is a lazy bouncer who says, *"As long as you're not in Bob's booth, sit wherever you want."* ArcFace is an elite bouncer who says, *"Everyone from Alice's family must sit shoulder-to-shoulder in a tight circle, AND there must be a mandatory 20-degree empty perimeter around your circle before anyone else can sit down."*

How ArcFace achieves this mathematically:
1. **$L_2$ Normalization:** We normalize both features and weights to unit length:
   $$||\mathbf{x}|| = 1, \quad ||\mathbf{w}_j|| = 1 \implies \mathbf{w}_j^T \mathbf{x} = \cos(\theta_j)$$
   Now, all embeddings live on the surface of a unit hypersphere. Magnitude is eliminated; **only angle matters**.
2. **Additive Angular Margin ($m = 0.35\text{ rad} \approx 20^\circ$):**  
   During training, we artificially make the angle larger for the true class:
   $$\cos(\theta_{y_i} + m) = \cos(\theta_{y_i})\cos(m) - \sin(\theta_{y_i})\sin(m)$$
3. **The ArcFace Loss Formulation:**
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ArcFace}} = -\frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \log \frac{e^{s \cdot \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m)}}{e^{s \cdot \cos(\theta_{y_i} + m)} + \sum_{j \neq y_i} e^{s \cdot \cos(\theta_j)}}$$
   *(where $s=30.0$ scales the hypersphere radius so gradients remain strong).*

### 7.3 What Happens at Test Time?
The ArcFace classification layer $\mathbf{W}$ is **thrown away**!  
The embedded device only saves the 48-D normalized embedding $\mathbf{e}$. Verification is a single line of C code:
$$\text{Similarity} = \mathbf{e}_{\text{live}} \cdot \mathbf{e}_{\text{stored}} = \sum_{d=1}^{48} e_{\text{live}}[d] \cdot e_{\text{stored}}[d] = \cos(\theta)$$
- If $\cos(\theta) \ge 0.74 \implies \theta \le 42.2^\circ \implies$ **ACCESS GRANTED**.
- If $\cos(\theta) < 0.74 \implies$ **ACCESS DENIED**.

### 8.1 The Sports Car Analogy
> **Mental Model:** If you only look at two sports cars parked in a driveway, they might look nearly identical in size and shape (Voltage Amplitude $x[n]$). But when you put your foot on the gas and measure their **0 to 60 mph acceleration** (Velocity $\Delta x[n]$), you instantly discover that Car A has a twin-turbo V8 engine while Car B has an electric motor.

In human electrophysiology:
- **Stream 1 ($x[n]$ - Voltage):** Represents static chamber volume and physical electrode distance.
- **Stream 2 ($\Delta x[n] = x[n] - x[n-1]$ - First Difference $dV/dt$):** Represents the speed of the action potential wavefront racing through the specialized Purkinje conduction system.

### 8.2 Resolving Ambiguity

However, Person A's left bundle branch conducts electricity at $2.2\text{ m/s}$ ($dV/dt = 48.5\text{ mV/s}$), while Person B's conducts at $1.8\text{ m/s}$ ($dV/dt = 36.2\text{ mV/s}$).  
By stacking both streams into a $(2, 300)$ tensor, the very first convolutional layer filters **joint amplitude-velocity trajectories**, resolving ambiguities that blinded single-stream networks.

### 9.1 The Biological Reason: Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia (RSA)
Your heart is not an electronic clock; your autonomic nervous system constantly modulates your cardiac cycle:
- **Inhale:** Vagal tone decreases $\to$ Heart accelerates $\to$ Thoracic cavity expands, slightly moving the chest wall away from the heart.
- **Exhale:** Vagal tone increases $\to$ Heart decelerates $\to$ Chest wall relaxes.

A single beat captured during deep inhalation might have a slight electrical vector tilt, causing an embedding error with probability $\epsilon \approx 0.089$ (8.9%).

### 9.2 The Mathematical Proof: Why 8 Beats Exceeds 97%
When we aggregate $K$ beats, we compute the **normalized mean embedding**:
$$\bar{\mathbf{e}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{e}_k}{||\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{e}_k||}$$

1. **Constructive Identity vs. Destructive Noise:**
   - The true biometric signature vector $\mathbf{e}_{\text{true}}$ points in the same direction on the hypersphere across all beats. It adds **constructively** $\propto K$.
   - Transient biological noise (EMG twitches, breathing artifacts) points in random, uncorrelated directions. Zero-mean random vectors add **destructively** $\propto \sqrt{K}$.
   $$\text{SNR}_{\text{ensemble}} = \frac{K \cdot ||\mathbf{e}_{\text{true}}||}{\sqrt{K} \cdot \sigma_{\text{noise}}} = \sqrt{K} \cdot \text{SNR}_{\text{single}}$$
   For $K=8$ beats, the biometric signal-to-noise ratio improves by $\sqrt{8} \approx \mathbf{2.83\times}$!

2. **The Binomial Theorem of Jury Voting:**
   Under independent beat trials, the probability that an ensemble of $N$ beats makes an erroneous majority decision follows the binomial tail:
   $$P(\text{Majority Error}) = \sum_{k=\lceil N/2 \rceil}^N \binom{N}{k} \epsilon^k (1 - \epsilon)^{N-k}$$
   For single-beat error $\epsilon = 0.089$:
   - For $N=1$: $P(\text{Error}) = 8.92\%$
   - For $N=5$: $P(\text{Error}) \approx 0.81\%$
   - For $N=8$: $P(\text{Error}) \approx \mathbf{0.18\%}$  
   This mathematical cancellation of uncorrelated noise is why 8 beats easily pushes verification accuracy **past 97% to 97.19%**!

## 11. Embedded Hardware Realities: Bridging PyTorch to the RP2040 Cortex-M0+

### 11.1 The Hardware Platform: Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040)
- **CPU:** Dual ARM Cortex-M0+ cores @ 133 MHz.
- **FPU (Floating Point Unit):** **NONE.** (No hardware instructions for float addition or multiplication).
- **SRAM:** **264 KB** on-chip.
- **Flash:** **2 MB** QSPI Flash.

### 11.2 The Two-Model Deployment Decision: Why Deploy Single-Stream?
If Dual-Stream ResNet reaches 97.19%, **why did we deploy Single-Stream on the Pico?**

```
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
|    Single-Stream ECG_ResNet_SE     |     Dual-Stream ECG_ResNet-SE      |
|     [ EDGE DEPLOYMENT CHAMPION ]   |       [ RESEARCH FRONTIER ]        |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| Flash Footprint: 78.9 KB           | Flash Footprint: ~96.2 KB          |
| SRAM Activation Buffer: 72 KB      | SRAM Activation Buffer: 97 KB      |
| Peak Inference Time: 300.05 ms     | Peak Inference Time: ~420 ms       |
| Core 1 Duty Cycle: 30.0%           | Core 1 Duty Cycle: 42.0%           |
| Multi-Beat Buffer: Fits 10 beats   | Multi-Beat Buffer: Tight on SRAM   |
| 5-Beat Accuracy: 95.51%            | 5-Beat Accuracy: 96.63%            |
| Decision: Real-time edge champion  | Decision: Cloud/Gateway frontier   |
+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
```

1. **SRAM Budget Safety:** The RP2040 has 264 KB of SRAM. Single-stream requires only 72 KB for activation buffers, leaving 100+ KB free for the stack, heap, and serial ring buffers. Dual-stream requires 97 KB for activations alone, risking memory corruption during sudden spikes.
2. **Thermal & Duty Cycle Budget:** Human heartbeats occur every $800\dots1000\text{ ms}$.  
   - Single-stream takes **300.05 ms** $\implies$ Core 1 runs for 300 ms, then sleeps for 500–700 ms (**30% duty cycle**).
   - This keeps the chip cool, saves battery power, and guarantees zero frame-dropping!

### 11.3 Dual-Core Asymmetric Multiprocessing (AMP)
To guarantee that high-speed ADC sampling never misses a single heartbeat sample:
- **Core 0 (The Real-Time Guardian):** Runs the 360 Hz timer interrupt, samples the analog frontend, computes the causal IIR bandpass and 50 Hz notch filter difference equations, and executes the Pan-Tompkins QRS detector. It pushes segmented 300-sample beat windows into a lock-free circular FIFO.
- **Core 1 (The Neural Engine):** Stays asleep in `WFE` (Wait For Event) low-power mode. When Core 0 pushes a completed beat, it wakes Core 1. Core 1 runs the ResNet forward pass using the RP2040's `bootrom` software floating-point library, computes the 48-D embedding, and performs cosine verification.

### Q1: "Why did you use an ECG as a biometric? Can't someone just record my heart with a microphone or stethoscope?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** Testing whether you understand the difference between acoustic heart sounds and electrophysiology.  
> **Your Answer:**  
> An ECG is not an acoustic sound; it is an **internal electrophysiological action potential** generated by the depolarization of millions of cardiac myocytes. It cannot be captured from a distance with a microphone or camera. Furthermore, ECG biometrics provides **inherent liveness detection**: an attacker cannot present a latent print, a silicone dummy, or a 3D mask. If there is no living, pulsing heart generating an authentic P-QRS-T waveform, authentication instantly fails.

### Q2: "Why did the classical AC/DCT + SVM baseline only achieve 79%?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** Probing your understanding of feature engineering vs. deep learning.  
> **Your Answer:**  
> The classical pipeline relied on handcrafted features. It calculated the Autocorrelation (AC) of the ECG window to cancel heart-rate variation and then took the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) of the autocorrelation to extract 32 frequency coefficients.  
> **The fatal flaw is that autocorrelation completely discards phase information!** In cardiac electrophysiology, the relative time delay between the P-wave and the R-peak (PR interval) is a critical biometric marker. AC threw that away. Furthermore, an SVM with an RBF kernel cannot learn non-linear temporal invariants or adapt to physiological baseline drift across multiple days.

---

### Q3: "Why did the LSTM perform so catastrophically (49.17%), even worse than the SVM?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** Checking if you just blindly tried architectures or actually understand sequence modeling assumptions.  
> **Your Answer:**  
> LSTMs are designed for ordered sequential time series where step $t$ causally depends on step $t-1$ (like words in a sentence). In our baseline experiment, the LSTM was fed the 32 DCT coefficients. Frequency coefficients do not have a causal temporal order!  
> Furthermore, when LSTMs are fed raw waveforms, their recurrent gates struggle with long sequences (300 timesteps) without convolutional feature extractors. The gradients either exploded or vanished, and the sequential computation prevented the network from capturing localized morphological wave shapes.

---

### Q4: "Why was a plain 1D-CNN inferior to ResNet (71.37% vs 89.29%)?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** The core architecture comparison of your project.  
> **Your Answer:**  
> Two main reasons:  
> 1. **The Degradation Problem:** As layers deepen in a plain feedforward CNN, backpropagated gradients shrink exponentially ($\lim \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \to 0$). Early layers stop learning, preventing the network from resolving fine morphological fiducials.  
> 2. **Receptive Field and MaxPool Oversmoothing:** A plain CNN uses aggressive max-pooling to downsample the signal ($300 \to 150 \to 75 \to 1$). This destroys the precise phase alignment and sharp, high-frequency slew rates of the QRS complex. ResNet solves this through **identity skip connections ($\mathbf{y} = \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$)**, providing an unobstructed gradient highway that lets high-frequency features bypass deep transformations without distortion.

---

### Q5: "What is the physical meaning of the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** Testing your understanding of attention mechanisms in time-series data.  
> **Your Answer:**  
> In standard convolution, every filter channel is treated with equal importance. But in an ECG, different channels detect different cardiac phases (e.g., Channel 4 detects the sharp R-peak upstroke, while Channel 11 detects the slow T-wave).  
> The SE block performs **channel-wise attention**:
> 1. It **squeezes** temporal length into a channel-summary vector using Global Average Pooling.
> 2. It passes this through a two-layer bottleneck to compute dynamic weights $s_c \in [0, 1]$.
> 3. It **excites** (multiplies) the feature maps by these weights.  
> If a beat has high muscle noise on the baseline, the SE block dynamically downweights noise-sensitive channels and boosts the pristine morphological channels, improving accuracy by $+1.58\%$.

### Q6: "Why didn't you use standard Softmax Cross-Entropy loss?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** Checking if you understand the open-set biometric enrollment requirement.  
> **Your Answer:**  
> Standard Softmax is only suitable for **closed-set classification**, where the test subjects are already known during training. In a real-world biometric system, new users must be enrolled without retraining the neural network.  
> Softmax only forces classes to be *linearly separable*; it does not force them to be *intra-class compact*. Heartbeats from the same person can scatter across a wide area in feature space. We needed **Metric Learning**.

### Q7: "How does ArcFace solve the open-set enrollment problem?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** The core loss function defense.  
> **Your Answer:**  
> ArcFace maps all heartbeat embeddings onto the surface of a unit hypersphere by normalizing $||\mathbf{x}|| = 1$ and $||\mathbf{w}|| = 1$.  
> During training, it adds an **additive angular margin $m = 0.35\text{ radians} \approx 20^\circ$** directly to the target class angle: $\cos(\theta + m)$.  
> This forces the network to pull heartbeats from the same person into an ultra-tight angular cluster while pushing strangers at least $20^\circ$ away on the sphere. At test time, the classification head is discarded. We enroll a user by simply storing their 48-D normalized embedding vector, and verify them using a fast cosine similarity dot product.

Coupling morphology ($x[n]$) with electrophysiological kinetics ($\Delta x[n]$) resolves amplitude ambiguities and drove our single-beat accuracy to 91.08%.

### Q11: "Why did you use Depthwise-Separable Convolutions instead of standard Conv1D?"
> **Examiner's Intent:** Testing your awareness of embedded edge constraints.

> 1. A depthwise filter per channel ($C_{in} \times K$).
> 2. A $1 \times 1$ pointwise projection ($C_{in} \times C_{out}$).
